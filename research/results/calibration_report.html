Liquidity Zero-Cross Bearish: timeframe and profit-factor calibration

The EA's rules replayed in Python on 480,000 real M1 bars. A first pass covered 6 trigger/bias timeframe pairs and 5,838 parameter sets. A wider second search covered 13 pairs and 126,360 sets, using the new v1.10 inputs. Fills use the broker's hourly bid/ask spread. Results are in R, multiples of the risk per trade. Because the EA sizes every trade to the same % of equity, profit factor (PF) in R matches PF in money.

Neighbours of the calibrated set, without trailing (RR ≥ 1, overlap session). The edge sits at lookback 25 with swing strength 3 and fades at lookback 40. Without trailing, most of the profit comes from 2026, a single strong period. Treat it as fragile.

What this tells you

    Method and limits

    • Data: FTMO-Server4 XAUUSD, mid-price M1 bars resampled from ticks, Jan 2 2025 – May 15 2026, in UTC. Higher timeframes are resampled from M1.
    • Fills: shorts enter at the bid on the first tick after the signal bar. SL and TP trigger on the ask. When one bar touches both SL and TP, the SL is counted first, the pessimistic choice. A gap through a level fills at the bar open.
    • Not modelled: the news filter (no calendar history), commission, swap, slippage beyond the spread, and the trailing step (trailing is continuous).
    • Point inputs are scaled by √(trigger minutes) on higher timeframes (buffer, trailing, SL bounds), so M5 and M15 aren't judged with M1-sized stops.
    • Sample sizes are small, roughly 3–8 trades a month. The 90% bootstrap interval for the calibrated M1/M15 set is PF 1.0–3.1, so this is evidence to test further in MT5, not proof.
